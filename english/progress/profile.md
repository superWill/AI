## Accent policy (decided 2026-07-29) — split by purpose, do not unify

The learner wants a **General American** accent for production. This is a deliberate,
accepted choice. Do not argue with it again.

- **Input / listening practice** → stay **British-weighted** (BBC, Cambridge audio).
  IELTS Listening is British/Australian/NZ dominant. An all-American listening diet
  costs listening band. This is not negotiable.
- **Output / shadowing / pronunciation drills** → **American**. Rationale is real life,
  not the test: his actual English use (CNBC, earnings calls, Fed pressers, US podcasts)
  is ~95% American.
- **Scoring**: IELTS Pronunciation rewards *intelligibility* — sounds, word and sentence
  stress, intonation, chunking. Accent choice is band-neutral. Never imply that sounding
  American raises the band. It does not.

Priority within pronunciation work, highest impact first (Chinese L1):
1. **Stress-timing / rhythm** — outweighs every phoneme issue combined
2. **Final consonants** — dropped -ed / -s destroys tense and plural signals (hits GRA too)
3. **Consonant clusters** — strengths, texts, asked
4. Only then: rhoticity, T-flapping, /æ/ vs /ɑː/

Cap pronunciation work at ~15 min/day. It is the most satisfying and least
band-efficient activity available; vocabulary is the live bottleneck.
